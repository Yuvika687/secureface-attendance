How it works

Attendance is marked at one shared scanner. Before it checks who you are, it checks that a real, live person is in front of the camera and not a photo, phone or screen.

The scan, step by step

Camera frameThe scanner sends one full webcam frame at a time.
Find the faceBlazeFace finds faces. No face or more than one face: stop and ask again.
Same face?The face box must overlap the previous frame's box (IoU). A jump means a new person, so start over.
CropFace plus a 25% margin, resized to 224×224, exactly as in training.
Liveness checkThe trained model scores how likely the face is a spoof, averaged over the last 5 frames.
Only real faces continueSPOOF or UNCERTAIN stops here. Recognition never runs on them.
Who is it?Face Mesh aligns the face; ArcFace compares it with registered students.
Random challengeTurn your head the way it asks (left or right, chosen at random), then blink once. The server checks both.
AttendanceShows name + student ID with "Attendance marked" or "Already marked today".
LIVE: real face SPOOF: photo / screen / video UNCERTAIN: hold still, retry NO FACE

Why a trained model for anti-spoofing?

Hand-written rules (texture, screen patterns, depth guesses) break easily: lighting, camera quality and new attack types all change what a "fake" looks like. A trained model learns those differences from thousands of labeled real and spoof examples instead.
